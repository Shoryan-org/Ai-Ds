# During Donation — What to Expect

## Registration and Health Screening

Before the donation begins:

- Staff confirm your identity
- You complete a confidential health questionnaire
- A mini-physical checks your:
  - Blood pressure
  - Pulse
  - Temperature
  - Hemoglobin level (finger-prick test)

## The Donation Procedure

**Step 1: Skin Preparation**
A phlebotomist locates a suitable vein and cleans the area on your arm with an antiseptic sponge.

**Step 2: Needle Insertion**
A brand-new, sterile, single-use needle is inserted into a vein in your arm. You may feel a brief pinch — similar to a standard vaccine or blood test — but the donation itself is not painful.

**Step 3: Blood Collection**
For a standard whole blood donation, approximately 450–500 mL (about one pint) is collected into a specialized plastic bag.

**Step 4: Timing**
- **Whole blood**: The actual blood draw takes 8–10 minutes
- **Apheresis (platelet or plasma)**: Takes 1–2 hours

**Step 5: Monitoring**
Medical staff monitor you throughout the process to ensure you remain comfortable.

## During the Draw

- Relax in the reclining chair
- You may be asked to gently pump your hand to help blood flow
- If you feel lightheaded, nauseous, or any discomfort, tell staff immediately

## Post-Draw Steps

- The needle is carefully removed
- Gentle pressure is applied to the site
- A sterile bandage is placed over the area
