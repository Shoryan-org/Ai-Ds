# Emergency Blood Donation

## The Role of Blood During Emergencies

During natural disasters, mass casualty events, or severe local accidents, the demand for blood can spike dramatically. Blood cannot be manufactured — it can only come from volunteer donors.

## Why O-Negative Is Crucial

- In extreme emergencies, there is no time to test a bleeding trauma patient's blood type
- **Type O-negative** red blood cells are the "universal donor" type and can be safely transfused into any patient
- Hospitals try to maintain a steady stock of O-negative blood

## Donating Proactively

- The blood that saves lives in an emergency is the blood **already on hospital shelves**
- Processing and testing donated blood takes up to 48 hours
- Rushing to donate the day of a disaster is helpful for restocking, but **maintaining a regular donation schedule** ensures hospitals are prepared before tragedy strikes

## What You Can Do

- Pre-register with your blood service
- Keep your contact information up to date
- Donate regularly — ideally 2–4 times a year for whole blood
- Respond to appeals when called

## Safety During Emergency Drives

Health screening and testing remain unchanged even in a crisis. Safety is **never compromised**. There are no "emergency shortcuts" for donor screening.
