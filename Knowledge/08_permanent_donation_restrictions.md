# Permanent Donation Restrictions

## What Is a Permanent Deferral?

A permanent restriction means you are not safely able to donate blood at any point in your life. These rules protect the blood supply from incurable infectious diseases and protect donors from potential health complications.

## Infectious Diseases

You are permanently restricted if you have ever:

- Tested positive for **HIV/AIDS**
- Been a carrier of **Hepatitis B** or **Hepatitis C**
- Been diagnosed with **HTLV** or **syphilis** (depending on country)

## Substance Use

- **Intravenous drug use** — ever injected non-prescription or recreational drugs

## Cancers

- **Blood cancers** (leukemia, lymphoma, multiple myeloma): Permanent deferral
- **Most solid tumor cancers**: Require a cancer-free waiting period (often 5 years) before eligibility may be reconsidered

## Neurological Conditions

- **Creutzfeldt-Jakob disease (CJD)** or family history
- **Variant CJD (vCJD)** risk from certain travel/residence
- **Dura mater transplant** or human pituitary-derived growth hormone

## Other Permanent Restrictions

- **Chagas disease** or **Babesiosis** (chronic infection)
- **Ebola** diagnosis
- **Organ or tissue transplant** (varies by country)

## Changes in Guidelines

Restrictions evolve as medical science advances. For example, many countries have recently lifted permanent bans related to vCJD for UK residents. Always verify current restrictions with your local blood authority.
