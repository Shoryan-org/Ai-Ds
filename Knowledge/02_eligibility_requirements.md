# Blood Donation Eligibility Requirements

## Basic Criteria

To donate blood, you must be in good general health and meet basic requirements. While specific rules vary by country, the core standards are similar worldwide.

## Age

- **Minimum age**: Typically 17 years. Some countries allow 16-year-olds with parental consent.
- **Maximum age**: Varies by country. Some countries set an upper limit of 60–65 for first-time donors.
- **Repeat donors**: May continue donating beyond the upper age limit if healthy and approved by a physician.

## Weight

- **Minimum weight**: At least 50 kg (110 lbs) for standard whole blood donation.
- **Lower volume donations**: In some countries, donors weighing at least 45 kg may donate 350 mL.

## General Health

You must:

- Feel well on the day of donation
- Have no active cold, flu, fever, sore throat, or infection
- Not be currently taking antibiotics for an active infection

## Hemoglobin (Iron) Levels

A finger-prick test checks your hemoglobin level before every donation.

- **Women**: Minimum of 12.0 g/dL (120 g/L)
- **Men**: Minimum of 13.0 g/dL (130 g/L)

If your hemoglobin is too low, you will be deferred and advised to eat iron-rich foods before trying again.

## Identification

Bring a valid photo ID — driver's license, passport, or official donor card.

## Donation Frequency

Waiting periods protect donor health by allowing the body to replenish iron stores:

- **United States (American Red Cross)**: Whole blood every 56 days (8 weeks), up to 6 times per year
- **United Kingdom (NHS)**: Men every 12 weeks, women every 16 weeks
- **WHO guidance**: Generally recommends a minimum of 8 weeks between donations
