# Vaccinations and Blood Donation

## Overview

Vaccines are encouraged for public health, but depending on the type of vaccine, you may need to wait before donating blood.

## No Waiting Period

If you are symptom-free and fever-free, you can generally donate immediately after receiving **inactivated (non-live) vaccines**:

- Seasonal flu shot (inactivated)
- COVID-19 mRNA vaccines (Pfizer, Moderna)
- COVID-19 viral-vector vaccines (Johnson & Johnson)
- Tetanus, diphtheria, pertussis (Tdap)
- Pneumococcal vaccine
- Meningococcal vaccine
- HPV (Human Papillomavirus)
- Hepatitis A and B

## Short Waiting Periods

**Live attenuated vaccines** require a temporary deferral to ensure the live virus is cleared from the bloodstream:

| Vaccine | Waiting Period |
|---------|---------------|
| Measles, Mumps, Rubella (MMR) | 4 weeks |
| Varicella (Chickenpox) | 4 weeks |
| Shingles (live Zostavax) | 4 weeks |
| Yellow fever | 2–4 weeks |
| Oral polio (Sabin) | 2–4 weeks |
| Nasal flu spray (LAIV) | 2 weeks |
| BCG (tuberculosis) | 4 weeks |

## Exceptions

If you receive a vaccine due to direct exposure to a disease (such as Hepatitis B vaccine after a needle-stick injury, or rabies after a dog bite), waiting periods may extend up to a year.

## COVID-19 Vaccine

After any authorized COVID-19 vaccine, if you feel well and have no fever, you can donate immediately — **no deferral is required**.
