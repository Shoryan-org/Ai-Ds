# Glossary of Blood Donation Terms

**Antibody**
A protein produced by the immune system that reacts with foreign antigens. Important in blood group compatibility.

**Antigen**
A substance (usually a protein or carbohydrate) on the surface of red blood cells that defines blood groups (e.g., A, B, Rh antigens).

**Apheresis**
A donation procedure using a machine to collect a specific blood component (platelets, plasma, or red cells) and return the rest to the donor.

**Blood Component**
A product separated from whole blood: red cells, platelets, plasma, or cryoprecipitate.

**Blood Group / Blood Type**
Classification based on antigens (e.g., A, B, AB, O) and Rh factor (positive or negative).

**Crossmatch**
A laboratory test to confirm compatibility between donor blood and patient blood before transfusion.

**Deferral**
A temporary or permanent restriction preventing a person from donating blood.

**Donor Screening**
The process of evaluating a donor's health history and physical condition to ensure safe donation.

**Hemoglobin (Hb)**
The iron-containing protein in red cells that carries oxygen. A minimum level is required to donate.

**Hematocrit**
The proportion of blood volume occupied by red blood cells; used to assess eligibility.

**Hemovigilance**
The surveillance system covering the entire blood transfusion chain to monitor and improve safety.

**Iron Depletion**
A reduction in body iron stores, which can occur in frequent donors; managed with dietary advice or supplements.

**Platelet**
Small blood cell fragments that help clotting. Platelet donations are often done via apheresis.

**Plasma**
The liquid portion of blood containing water, proteins, antibodies, and clotting factors. Used for burn victims, trauma, and coagulation disorders.

**Rh Factor**
The D antigen on red cells. People are either Rh-positive or Rh-negative.

**Universal Donor**
- **Red blood cells**: Type O-negative (safe for all recipients)
- **Plasma**: Type AB

**Universal Recipient**
Type AB-positive — can receive red blood cells from any blood type.

**Vasovagal Reaction**
A common fainting-like response during or after donation, triggered by a drop in heart rate and blood pressure.

**Whole Blood**
Blood collected without separation, containing red cells, white cells, platelets, and plasma.
