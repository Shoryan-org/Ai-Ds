# Travel Restrictions and Blood Donation

## Why Travel Affects Donation

Infections such as **Malaria**, **Zika**, and **Dengue** are transmitted by mosquitoes in certain regions. These parasites and viruses can remain undetected in your bloodstream and be transmitted to a patient via transfusion.

## Malaria-Endemic Regions

If you travel to a country where malaria is found:

- Typical deferral: **3 to 12 months** after returning, depending on regional guidelines
- If you previously lived in a malaria-endemic country for an extended period: deferral can be up to **3 years**
- If you are diagnosed with malaria: extended waiting period after being fully cured

**US (American Red Cross)**: A 3-month travel deferral is used for travel to malaria-endemic areas.

## vCJD (Mad Cow Disease)

Historically, many countries banned donors who spent time in the **UK and Europe during the 1980s and 1990s** due to Variant Creutzfeldt-Jakob Disease (vCJD).

- Some countries have recently lifted this restriction
- Others maintain it
- Always check your local blood authority

## Other Disease Risks

- **Chagas disease** risk areas (Central and South America): May require testing; permanent deferral if positive
- **Zika virus**: Temporary deferrals based on outbreak status
- **Ebola**: Temporary deferrals during active outbreaks

## What to Do

When registering on the Shoryan app, have your recent international travel dates and locations ready for the donor questionnaire.
