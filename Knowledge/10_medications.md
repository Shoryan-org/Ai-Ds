# Medications and Blood Donation

## Overview

Most common medications do **not** disqualify you from donating blood. Your eligibility depends more on **why** you take the medication than the medication itself.

**Never stop taking a prescribed medication just to donate blood.**

## Generally Accepted Medications

These medications typically do **not** prevent donation:

- Blood pressure medications (ACE inhibitors, beta blockers, diuretics)
- Cholesterol-lowering drugs (statins)
- Thyroid replacement hormones
- Oral contraceptives and hormone replacement therapy
- Most antidepressants (SSRIs, SNRIs)
- Insulin (with diabetes criteria)
- Allergy medications (antihistamines)
- Pain relievers (paracetamol, ibuprofen)

## Medications with Waiting Periods

| Medication | Deferral Period | Reason |
|------------|-----------------|--------|
| **Isotretinoin** (Accutane, Absorica) | 1 month | Risk of birth defects if transfused to pregnant woman |
| **Finasteride** (Propecia, Proscar) | 1 month | Risk of birth defects |
| **Dutasteride** (Avodart) | 6 months | Risk of birth defects |
| **Acitretin** (Soriatane) | 3 years | Risk of birth defects |
| **Antibiotics** | Finish course + symptom-free | Active infection |
| **Aspirin** (platelet donors only) | 48–72 hours | Affects platelet function |
| **Blood thinners** (warfarin, apixaban) | Varies | Donor bleeding risk |
| **Oral PrEP/PEP** | 3 months | HIV prevention drugs |
| **Injectable PrEP** | 2 years | HIV prevention drugs |

## Medications That May Cause Permanent Deferral

- Human pituitary-derived growth hormone
- Certain immunosuppressants (depending on country)
