# Blood Donation Myths — Facts vs. Fiction

## Myth: Donating blood is painful and takes a long time.

**Fact**: The needle insertion is a quick pinch. The draw itself is painless and takes under 10 minutes. The whole visit is about an hour.

## Myth: I can get a disease from donating.

**Fact**: All equipment is sterile, single-use, and discarded. There is **zero risk** of contracting HIV, hepatitis, or any infection from the donation process.

## Myth: Vegetarians can't donate because of low iron.

**Fact**: Many vegetarians have healthy iron levels. Eating iron-rich plant foods (lentils, spinach, fortified cereals) with vitamin C helps absorption. Hemoglobin is tested before each donation.

## Myth: Older people can't give blood.

**Fact**: There is usually **no upper age limit** for regular donors if they are healthy. Some countries have an upper age for first-time donors (e.g., 60–65), but many regular donors continue well into their 70s.

## Myth: If I have high blood pressure, I can't donate.

**Fact**: Controlled hypertension is acceptable. Blood pressure is checked; as long as readings are within accepted limits (below 180/100), you can donate.

## Myth: Donating makes you gain weight.

**Fact**: Donating blood has no effect on body weight or metabolism.

## Myth: You can't donate if you have diabetes.

**Fact**: Type 1 and type 2 diabetes are allowed if blood sugar is well-controlled and there are no complications.

## Myth: My blood type is common, so it isn't needed.

**Fact**: Common types like O+ and A+ are needed **most** because more patients have those types. Every type is in demand.

## Myth: If I've been deferred once, I'm permanently banned.

**Fact**: Many deferrals are **temporary**. You can often return after the waiting period. Staff will explain your eligibility.
