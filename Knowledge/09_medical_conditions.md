# Medical Conditions and Blood Donation

## Overview

Having a medical condition does **not** automatically disqualify you from donating blood. If a condition is well-controlled and you meet general health requirements, you may still be eligible.

## Generally Acceptable Conditions

**Asthma**
Acceptable if well-managed with no current shortness of breath or active wheezing.

**High Blood Pressure (Hypertension)**
Acceptable if blood pressure is **below 180/100** at the time of donation. Taking blood pressure medications does not disqualify you.

**Diabetes**
- Acceptable if well-controlled through diet or oral medications
- Insulin-dependent diabetics may be eligible depending on the country

**Thyroid Disease**
Generally acceptable if hormone levels are stable.

## Conditions Requiring Evaluation

**Heart Disease**
History of heart attack or bypass surgery usually requires a waiting period (often 6 months) and medical evaluation.

**Cancer**
- **Solid-tumor cancers** successfully treated: Often require a waiting period (1–5 years)
- **Blood cancers** (leukemia, lymphoma): Permanent deferral

**Epilepsy**
May be allowed if seizure-free for a specified period (rules vary by country).

## Medications and Medical Conditions

Most medications for chronic conditions — including insulin, blood pressure pills, and asthma inhalers — do not prevent donation. However, some medications may require a waiting period (see medications document).

## Important Note

If you have a condition not listed, ask your local blood center. Always disclose your full medical history during screening.
