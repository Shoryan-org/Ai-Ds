# Blood Donation Overview

## What Is Blood Donation?

Blood donation is a voluntary medical procedure in which a healthy person gives blood or blood components for use in transfusions. Donated blood is essential for saving lives during emergencies, surgeries, cancer treatments, childbirth complications, and severe anemia.

There is **no artificial substitute** for human blood. Patients rely entirely on volunteer donors.

## How Donations Save Lives

A single donation can save **up to three lives**. Donated blood is separated into three main components, each used for different medical needs:

- **Red blood cells** — carry oxygen to tissues; used for trauma, surgery, and anemia
- **Platelets** — help blood clot; vital for cancer patients and those with bleeding disorders
- **Plasma** — the liquid portion of blood; used for burn victims, trauma, and clotting disorders

## Types of Blood Donation

**Whole Blood Donation**
The most common type. Approximately 450–500 mL (about one pint) is collected. The actual blood draw takes 8–10 minutes, and the entire appointment lasts about one hour.

**Apheresis (Automated Donation)**
A machine collects a specific component (platelets, plasma, or red cells) and returns the remaining blood to the donor. This takes longer — typically 1 to 2 hours.

- **Platelet donation** — can be done more frequently (up to 24 times per year)
- **Plasma donation** — collected via apheresis
- **Double red cell (Power Red)** — two units of red cells collected, plasma and platelets returned

## Why Blood Is Always Needed

Blood components have a limited shelf life:

- Red blood cells: up to 42 days
- Platelets: 5–7 days
- Plasma: can be frozen for up to one year

This means a constant supply of donors is essential. In England alone, approximately **5,000 donations** are needed every day.

## How the Body Recovers

Donating blood is safe for healthy adults. The body quickly replaces the donated volume:

- **Plasma volume** is restored within 24–48 hours
- **Red blood cells** are fully replenished within 4–6 weeks
