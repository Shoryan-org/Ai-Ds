# Temporary Donation Restrictions

## What Is a Temporary Deferral?

A temporary restriction (deferral) means you cannot donate blood right now but will be eligible again once a specific waiting period has passed. These rules protect both donor and recipient.

## Illness and Infection

- **Cold, flu, sore throat, fever**: Wait until fully recovered and symptom-free
- **Antibiotics for infection**: Finish the full course and feel well before donating

## Tattoos and Body Piercings

- **WHO guidance**: Defer for **6 months** from the date of the procedure
- **If performed by a registered health professional and inflammation has settled**: May donate after **12 hours**
- **Country variations**: In some regulated settings, no wait is required; in others, 3–6 months

## Dental Work (Procedures, Fillings, Extractions)

- **Minor procedures** (fillings, cleaning, check-ups, routine dental work): Wait **24 hours** after the appointment.
- **Major dental surgery** (extractions, root canals, implants, gum surgery): Wait **one month**.
- If you have an active tooth infection or abscess, you must finish the full course of antibiotics and be symptom-free before donating.

## Vaccinations

- **Inactivated/killed vaccines** (flu shot, COVID-19 mRNA, tetanus): No wait if feeling well
- **Live attenuated vaccines** (MMR, chickenpox, yellow fever): Typically 2–4 weeks

## Travel

- **Malaria-endemic areas**: Defer for 3–12 months depending on destination and exposure
- **vCJD risk**: Policies vary by country (see travel restrictions document)

## Pregnancy

- **During pregnancy**: Cannot donate at any stage
- **After childbirth**: Waiting periods vary (see pregnancy document)

## Low Hemoglobin

If your iron test is low on the day of donation, you will be deferred. Eat iron-rich foods and try again later.
