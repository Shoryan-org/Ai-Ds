# Pregnancy, Breastfeeding, and Blood Donation

## During Pregnancy

Women **cannot donate blood at any point during pregnancy**. The mother's body requires high levels of iron and red blood cells to support fetal development.

## After Pregnancy (Postpartum)

Waiting periods vary by country:

- **WHO recommendation**: Defer for as many months as the duration of the pregnancy. For a full-term pregnancy, wait **at least 9 months**
- **American Red Cross**: Wait **6 weeks** after giving birth (vaginal delivery), provided there were no complications
- **NHS (UK)**: Wait **6 months** after delivery

If you received a blood transfusion during delivery, the deferral period is extended (often 3 to 12 months, depending on local regulations).

## While Breastfeeding

- **WHO**: Advises against donating blood while actively breastfeeding. Wait until **3 months after the baby is significantly weaned** to solid foods
- **American Red Cross**: May allow donation during breastfeeding if the mother meets all weight and hemoglobin requirements

## Key Health Considerations

- Pregnancy and breastfeeding increase iron needs
- Donation can worsen iron depletion
- A recent hemoglobin test is essential before donation

## Country Variations

Rules differ significantly. Always check your local blood service guidelines. The screening questionnaire will cover dates of last delivery and breastfeeding status.
